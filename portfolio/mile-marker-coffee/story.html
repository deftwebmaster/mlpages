Since the Road

The road was here first.

Mile Marker Coffee started with a boarded-up service station, a stubborn sense that the building still had a job to do, and travelers who kept asking where else they should stop.

Alpine 214 Marfa

Retired Pumps

Small counter, long memory.

The station opened in the 1960s as a practical place: fuel, oil, cold soda, a pay phone that only worked when it felt like it, and shade for anyone trying to cross West Texas without losing patience.

By the late nineties the pumps were quiet. The building sat almost empty until a small renovation brought back the tile, the canopy, and the big windows. The first version of Mile Marker Coffee had six seats, one espresso machine, and a roasting drum tucked behind a wall that smelled permanently like cocoa and toast.

Travelers carried the story farther than any sign could. By 2022, the roasting room opened behind the cafe, and Mile Marker 214 became the kind of stop people planned into the drive instead of discovering by accident.
